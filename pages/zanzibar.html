About Zanzibar

The Spice Island of East Africa

Zanzibar is an archipelago of islands off the coast of mainland Tanzania, combining natural beauty, cultural richness and an extraordinary marine environment. The main island — Unguja — is home to Stone Town, dozens of beach villages, and some of the world's most pristine coral reefs.

Once the centre of the East African spice and slave trade, Zanzibar's layered history blends Arab, Persian, Indian, African and Portuguese influences into a uniquely captivating culture. At Seresafari, we pair your Tanzania wildlife adventure with a curated Zanzibar extension — combining bush thrills with island paradise in one seamless trip.

Pristine White-Sand Beaches
Nungwi, Kendwa, Paje, Matemwe, Jambiani — each beach has a distinct character, from lively resort towns to untouched, remote stretches of sand.
Stone Town — UNESCO World Heritage
A living museum of narrow alleys, carved doorways, bustling markets and crumbling palaces — Stone Town is one of East Africa's most atmospheric urban destinations.
Spice Farm Tours
Walk through fragrant gardens of cloves, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg, black pepper, cardamom and ylang-ylang on a guided tour of Zanzibar's legendary spice farms.
Diving & Snorkelling
World-class coral reefs shelter sea turtles, whale sharks, dolphins, octopus, reef sharks and hundreds of colourful tropical fish species in the warm Indian Ocean.

Northern Tip Most Popular

Nungwi

The most vibrant beach village on the island, Nungwi sits at Zanzibar's northern tip where the tide stays high and swimming is good at all hours. A lively mix of boutique resorts, beach bars, traditional dhow builders and a beautiful natural aquarium make this a favourite for those wanting the full Zanzibar experience.

East Coast Kite Capital

Paje

On Zanzibar's east coast, Paje is the island's kitesurfing capital — a long stretch of white sand backed by swaying palms and shallow turquoise lagoons. Perfect for watersports enthusiasts, Paje also attracts those simply seeking a quieter, more laid-back beach experience away from the crowds of the north.

World-class kitesurfing & windsurfing

North-East Coast Luxury & Romantic

Matemwe

Matemwe is one of Zanzibar's most beautiful and undeveloped stretches of beach, with crystal-clear water, fine white sand and a small traditional fishing village. Home to some of the island's most exclusive boutique lodges, Matemwe is popular with honeymooners and those seeking tranquility, privacy and spectacular Indian Ocean views from the comfort of a secluded retreat.

Things to Do

Zanzibar Experiences

Beyond the beach, Zanzibar offers a rich tapestry of cultural, culinary and oceanic experiences that make it one of Africa's most complete island destinations.

Snorkelling & Diving
Explore Zanzibar's extraordinary coral reefs at Mnemba Atoll, Leven Bank and Chumbe Island. Dive with green and hawksbill sea turtles, whale sharks (October–February), reef sharks and vibrant tropical fish.
Stone Town Walking Tour
Guided walk through the UNESCO World Heritage Site — visit the House of Wonders, the Old Fort, the Slave Market memorial, the Darajani spice market, and the beautifully restored Freddie Mercury House.
Spice Farm Tour
A half-day guided tour through working spice farms in Zanzibar's interior. Smell, taste and identify cloves, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg, lemongrass, turmeric, and more in their natural growing environment.
Sunset Dhow Cruise
Sail into the golden sunset aboard a traditional Zanzibari dhow. Fresh seafood barbecue on board, sundowners as the sky turns amber, and the silhouette of Stone Town disappearing into the horizon.
Dolphin Tours
Head to Kizimkazi on the south-west coast for the chance to swim alongside wild spinner dolphins in their natural habitat. Morning tours offer the best opportunities for close encounters in calm conditions.
Forodhani Night Market
Every evening, Stone Town's Forodhani Gardens transforms into a vibrant night food market — fresh Zanzibar pizza, sugar cane juice, grilled seafood, samosas and Swahili street food cooked right before your eyes.
Fishing with Locals
Join Zanzibar's traditional fishermen on an early-morning fishing trip in their wooden ngalawa outrigger canoes. An authentic, immersive experience that connects you directly with island life and the Indian Ocean.
Sea Turtle Sanctuary
Visit the Nungwi Natural Aquarium, a tidal lagoon sanctuary home to endangered green and hawksbill sea turtles. Swim alongside these gentle giants in their protected natural environment — a magical experience for all ages.
UNESCO Heritage

Stone Town — History Lives Here

Stone Town is the old city and cultural heart of Zanzibar, a UNESCO World Heritage Site that has changed remarkably little over the last two centuries. Its winding alleyways, coral stone buildings, ornate carved wooden doors and historic palaces tell the story of centuries of Arab, Persian, Indian and Swahili cultural exchange.

Walk where sultans, explorers and traders once walked. See where David Livingstone and Henry Morton Stanley met before their famous expeditions into the African interior. Visit the sobering site of the East African slave market — one of the continent's most important and poignant historical landmarks.

Carved Doors

Over 500 intricately carved Zanzibari doors — each a unique work of art blending Arab, Indian and African motifs.

Slave Market

The site of East Africa's last open slave market, now an Anglican cathedral and memorial — a moving and important historical site.

Freddie Mercury

The legendary Queen frontman was born in Stone Town in 1946. Visit his childhood home, now a museum and boutique hotel.

Darajani Market

Zanzibar's most vibrant local market, overflowing with fresh fish, tropical fruits, spices and the bustle of daily island life.

When to Go

Best Time to Visit Zanzibar

Zanzibar enjoys a tropical climate year-round with temperatures between 26–32°C. Two distinct dry seasons offer the best conditions for beach holidays.

June – October
Best Season

The long dry season brings cool sea breezes, brilliant sunshine and calm, crystal-clear waters — perfect for diving, snorkelling and beach relaxation. Visibility underwater can exceed 30 metres. The coolest and most comfortable time of year, with temperatures around 26–28°C.

December – February
Short Dry Season

The short dry season is warm, sunny and excellent for beach holidays. December–January is peak whale shark season around Pemba Channel. Water temperatures are at their warmest. Popular with European travellers escaping winter — book early for this period.

March – May
Long Rains Season

April–May sees Zanzibar's heaviest rainfall, with short but intense tropical downpours — often in the afternoon. Prices are significantly lower and beaches are quieter. Some hotels and dive centres close during this period. Still warm and lush — a good option for budget travellers who don't mind occasional rain.
