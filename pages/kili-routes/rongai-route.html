Route Overview

The Rongai Route is the only route that approaches Kilimanjaro from the north — from the Kenyan border side — offering a completely different perspective of the mountain. It is characterised by drier conditions (less rainfall than the southern routes), fewer trekkers on the trail, and a gradual, consistent ascent gradient that makes it one of the more approachable routes for first-time high-altitude trekkers.

The northern approach passes through semi-arid moorland and grassland landscapes before reaching the arctic summit zone. Wildlife encounters in the lower sections — including buffalo, eland and various raptors — are more common than on the crowded southern routes. The descent is made via the Marangu Route, adding scenic variety to the circuit.

Seresafari recommends Rongai for climbers seeking a quieter experience, those who prefer drier conditions, and those who want a steadier climb gradient on the ascent. The 7-day option adds a vital acclimatisation day and significantly improves summit success rates.

Camp-by-Camp Itinerary (7 Days)
Day
1
Arusha → Rongai Gate → Simba Camp
Rongai Gate (1,950m) → Simba Camp (2,625m) | 4–5 hrs | 9 km

Transfer from Arusha to Rongai Gate near the Kenyan border (approximately 3 hours). Enter a dry, open landscape very different from the lush southern forests — semi-arid scrubland giving way to moorland as you gain altitude. The northern approach is quiet, beautiful and wildlife-rich in the lower sections — buffalo tracks and eland are commonly seen. Arrive at Simba Camp in the late afternoon with your first clear views of Kibo's summit to the south-west. Your chef prepares a hot dinner as your guide conducts the first altitude and health briefing of the climb.

Northern Approach Start Semi-Arid Moorland Buffalo & Eland Territory First Kibo Summit Views
Camp: Simba Camp (2,625m) | All Meals
Day
2
Simba Camp → Second Cave Camp
Simba Camp (2,625m) → Second Cave Camp (3,450m) | 4–5 hrs | 8 km

A gradual ascent through open moorland — the defining characteristic of the Rongai Route. The landscape is more expansive and less enclosed than the southern routes, with wide open views of the Kenyan plains below to the north and the Kibo summit massif growing larger to the south. Pass through heather zones with distinctive giant lobelia plants. Arrive at Second Cave Camp — a dramatic campsite at the base of the northern slopes named after the lava caves nearby. The Mawenzi Peak (5,149m) is clearly visible — Kilimanjaro's dramatic secondary volcanic summit.

Open Moorland Trekking Kenya Plains Views Mawenzi Peak in Sight Giant Lobelia Plants
Camp: Second Cave Camp (3,450m) | All Meals
Day
3
Second Cave → Third Cave (Acclimatisation Walk)
Second Cave (3,450m) → Third Cave (3,870m) + acclimatisation side walk → Camp | 5–6 hrs

Continue ascending through increasingly barren alpine landscape toward the Third Cave campsite. After arriving at camp, your guide leads an acclimatisation walk — climbing an additional 200–300m before descending to sleep ("climb high, sleep low"). This critical strategy conditions the body to altitude without permanent high-altitude exposure. The Mawenzi Peak becomes dramatically close from this vantage — its jagged volcanic spires against the sky are extraordinary. Weather patterns on the northern side are typically clearer and drier than the south, offering superb views.

Acclimatisation Walk Mawenzi Peak Close-Up Northern Side Clear Skies Altitude Adaptation
Camp: Third Cave Camp (3,870m) | All Meals
Day
4
Third Cave → School Hut / Kibo Hut
Third Cave (3,870m) → School Hut (4,750m) | 4–5 hrs | 8 km

A steady climb through the alpine desert zone — here the landscape becomes truly otherworldly: volcanic rock, scree, dust and almost no vegetation. The crater rim of Kibo grows imposingly large overhead. Pass the Mawenzi Tarn — a small glacial lake at 4,330m — before the final steep push to School Hut at 4,750m, the highest camp on the Rongai Route and the launch point for tonight's summit push. Rest, eat well, attend a detailed summit night briefing from your lead guide, and attempt to sleep from 6 PM onward. The alarm rings at midnight.

Mawenzi Tarn Glacier Lake Alpine Desert Zone Summit Night Briefing Rest Before Summit
Camp: School Hut (4,750m) | All Meals — Depart midnight
Day
5
SUMMIT NIGHT → Uhuru Peak → Horombo Hut
School Hut (4,750m) → Gillman's Point (5,685m) → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Horombo Hut (3,720m)

Midnight departure in complete darkness. The Rongai summit approach climbs via Gillman's Point on the crater rim — then traverses the crater rim to Uhuru Peak. Gillman's Point itself (5,685m) is a legitimate achievement — many consider this the summit for KINAPA certificate purposes. The additional 45-minute ridge walk to Uhuru Peak (5,895m) at 5,895m is highly recommended if health allows. Sunrise from the roof of Africa, with views across Tanzania, Kenya and the vast Indian Ocean horizon. Begin descent immediately — the long journey to Horombo Hut on the Marangu Route takes the rest of the day. Arrive exhausted but triumphant.

Midnight Summit Push Gillman's Point (5,685m) Uhuru Peak (5,895m) Descent via Marangu Route
Camp: Horombo Hut (3,720m) — Marangu Huts | All Meals
Day
6
Horombo Hut → Marangu Gate → Arusha
Horombo Hut (3,720m) → Mandara Hut (2,720m) → Marangu Gate (1,830m) | 5–7 hrs | 20 km

The final day — a long but beautiful descent through the Marangu Route's lush cloud forest. The contrast from yesterday's arctic summit to today's birdsong-filled forest is striking and deeply satisfying. Arrive at Marangu Gate where your official KINAPA summit certificates are awarded. The traditional tipping ceremony for your mountain crew follows — guides and porters gather for farewell songs in a moving and joyful celebration of the team's achievement together. Transfer to Arusha arriving mid-afternoon. Tonight: celebration, rest and memories that will last a lifetime.
