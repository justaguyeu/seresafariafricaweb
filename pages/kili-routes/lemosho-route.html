Route Overview

The Lemosho Route is widely considered the finest route on Kilimanjaro — offering the best combination of scenery, wildlife, acclimatisation and summit success rate. It approaches from the west through pristine wilderness, beginning in lush rainforest and traversing the entire magnificent Shira Plateau before joining the Machame Route above the Lava Tower.

The longer approach means fewer crowds in the first days, more time in the wilderness, and critically — more acclimatisation days. The 8-day option includes an extra acclimatisation day on the Shira Plateau which is highly recommended and delivers the mountain's highest summit success rates of any route — consistently above 95%.

Seresafari recommends the Lemosho Route for climbers who want the best possible summit experience, outstanding scenery and maximum acclimatisation. It is also the route we recommend for photographers, as the Shira Plateau and western approach offer unique perspectives unavailable on other routes.

Camp-by-Camp Itinerary (8 Days)
Day
1
Arusha → Lemosho Gate → Forest Camp
Lemosho Gate (2,100m) → Forest Camp (2,750m) | 3–4 hrs | 7 km

Transfer from Arusha to Lemosho Gate on the western slopes — a longer drive than Machame but through some of Tanzania's most beautiful highland scenery. Complete park registration and meet your full mountain crew. The trail enters one of Kilimanjaro's most pristine and least-trekked rainforest sections — buffalo, elephants and colobus monkeys inhabit these western forests. A relatively easy first day allows gentle acclimatisation. Arrive at Forest Camp before dusk for dinner and your guide's first health briefing.

Pristine Western Rainforest Buffalo & Elephant Territory Easy Acclimatisation Start
Camp: Big Tree Camp / Forest Camp (2,750m) | All Meals
Day
2
Forest Camp → Shira 1 Camp
Forest Camp (2,750m) → Shira 1 Camp (3,500m) | 5–7 hrs | 9 km

Exit the rainforest into heath and moorland — a dramatic landscape transition as giant heather trees rise to 10 metres and the vegetation becomes wilder and more ethereal. The trail climbs steadily through moorland to the edge of the ancient Shira Plateau. Shira 1 Camp sits at the western edge of the plateau with the summit of Kibo dominating the horizon to the east. This is the beginning of Lemosho's unique advantage — the full plateau traverse.

Heath & Moorland Zone Shira Plateau Entry First Clear Kibo Summit Views
Camp: Shira 1 Camp (3,500m) | All Meals
Day
3
Shira 1 → Shira 2 Camp (Acclimatisation Walk)
Shira 1 (3,500m) → Shira Cathedral (3,872m, side trip) → Shira 2 Camp (3,840m) | 5–6 hrs | 9 km

Cross the full width of the Shira Plateau — one of the most magnificent high-altitude landscapes in Africa. An optional acclimatisation walk to Shira Cathedral (a dramatic rock formation at 3,872m) before continuing east to Shira 2 Camp. The plateau is home to unique high-altitude plants — giant lobelia, everlasting flowers, and otherworldly senecios. Eland antelope are occasionally spotted here. Arrive at Shira 2 Camp — the same camp reached by the Machame Route — with magnificent panoramic views of the summit massif.

Full Shira Plateau Traverse Shira Cathedral Side Trip Giant Senecios & Lobelias Eland on the Plateau
Camp: Shira 2 Camp (3,840m) | All Meals — (Extra acclimatisation day on 8-day option)
Day
4
Shira 2 → Lava Tower → Barranco Camp
Shira 2 (3,840m) → Lava Tower (4,630m) → Barranco Camp (3,950m) | 7–8 hrs | 10 km

The acclimatisation day shared with Machame Route — trek to the spectacular Lava Tower at 4,630m ("climb high, sleep low") before descending into the Barranco Valley. The Lava Tower is a dramatic 300m volcanic plug rising from the alpine desert — a striking geological formation in a surreal landscape. Lunch here at the highest point of the day, then descend to Barranco Camp significantly lower in altitude — the ideal acclimatisation strategy. Views of the Southern Ice Fields and Breach Wall are spectacular from this section of the route.

Lava Tower (4,630m) "Climb High Sleep Low" Day Southern Ice Field Views Alpine Desert Landscape
Camp: Barranco Camp (3,950m) | All Meals
Day
5
Barranco Camp → Karanga Camp
Barranco (3,950m) → Barranco Wall → Karanga Camp (4,035m) | 4–5 hrs | 5 km

The famous and exhilarating Barranco Wall — a 300m near-vertical scramble using hands and feet on solid volcanic rock. Despite its dramatic appearance, the wall is non-technical — your guides lead the way and assist at every tricky section. The reward at the top is a breathtaking panorama of Kilimanjaro's southern face, the Heim and Kersten Glaciers, and the vast plains below. Traverse the southern face to Karanga Valley and climb to Karanga Camp — well-positioned for tomorrow's push to high camp.

Barranco Wall Scramble Glacier & Ice Field Panoramas Southern Face Traverse
Camp: Karanga Camp (4,035m) | All Meals
Day
6
Karanga Camp → Barafu High Camp
Karanga (4,035m) → Barafu High Camp (4,673m) | 4–5 hrs | 4 km

A short but steep climb through the upper alpine desert to Barafu — the launch camp for every Machame and Lemosho summit bid. Arrive early afternoon to rest and acclimatise. Your lead guide delivers a detailed summit night briefing: departure time (midnight), clothing layering system, pace, breathing techniques, and symptoms to report immediately. Eat well, sleep early (from 5 PM), set multiple alarms. The summit is 1,200 vertical metres above — and the most important 6 hours of your Kilimanjaro journey begin at midnight.

Camp: Barafu High Camp (4,673m) | All Meals — Depart midnight
Day
7
SUMMIT NIGHT → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp
Barafu (4,673m) → Stella Point (5,739m) → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp (3,100m)

Midnight departure. Headlamps illuminate a single trail of climbers zig-zagging upward into the darkness. The temperature drops to -15°C or lower. Your guide sets a deliberate, very slow pace — breathing is everything now at altitude. After 5–6 hours of sustained climbing, the crater rim at Stella Point (5,739m) comes into view — the hardest psychological and physical moment. From Stella Point, traverse the crater rim for 45 minutes to Uhuru Peak. The sun rises as you approach the summit — an explosion of colour painting the horizon orange and gold above the clouds. At 5,895m you stand on the Roof of Africa. The emotion — exhaustion, elation, awe — is overwhelming. After photos and celebration, begin the long descent. Rest at Barafu, then continue down the Mweka trail to camp.

Midnight Summit Departure Stella Point (5,739m) — Crater Rim Uhuru Peak (5,895m) at Sunrise Descent to Mweka Camp
Camp: Mweka Camp (3,100m) | All Meals — Deepest sleep of your life!
Day
8
Mweka Camp → Mweka Gate → Arusha
Mweka Camp (3,100m) → Mweka Gate (1,640m) → Arusha | 3–4 hrs descent | 10 km

The final morning descent through lush rainforest — a beautiful and contemplative walk as summit memories settle in. Reach Mweka Gate where KINAPA rangers award your official Summit Certificate. The porter farewell ceremony — your mountain crew performing farewell songs with incredible energy despite the long climb — is deeply moving. Tipping ceremony follows before the transfer back to Arusha. Tonight belongs to celebration: a hot shower, a cold drink, and a story you'll tell for the rest of your life.

Why Choose Lemosho?

Best summit success rate: The 8-day Lemosho consistently delivers a 95%+ summit success rate — higher than any other route. The extended acclimatisation profile, with an extra day on the Shira Plateau, gives your body crucial time to adapt before the summit push.

Most complete experience: Lemosho traverses the full width of Kilimanjaro from west to east — covering more terrain and more ecological zones than any other route. You walk through pristine wilderness that sees a fraction of Machame's foot traffic in the early days.

Photography paradise: The western approach, Shira Plateau, and Shira Cathedral offer photographic perspectives unavailable on other routes. The giant lobelia and senecio plants of the Shira Plateau are among Kilimanjaro's most extraordinary subjects.

Fewer crowds: While the upper mountain (Barafu and above) is shared with Machame climbers, the first 3 days of Lemosho feel entirely private. The western approach trail is lightly used and wildlife encounters — including buffalo and elephant — are possible in the lower forest.
