Route Overview

The Marangu Route — nicknamed the "Coca-Cola Route" — is Kilimanjaro's oldest established route and the only route that uses hut accommodation rather than camping tents. Dormitory-style sleeping huts at each camp provide basic but solid shelter, making this route popular with climbers who prefer not to sleep in tents on the mountain.

The route ascends and descends the same south-eastern path — the only Kilimanjaro route that does not offer a circuit. This means climbers see the same scenery going up and down, which some find repetitive. However, the path is well-graded, clearly marked and consistently maintained — making it a reliable choice for first-time high-altitude trekkers.

Important: despite its reputation as the "easy" route, Marangu has the lowest summit success rate of any major Kilimanjaro route — primarily because its 5-day standard duration provides insufficient acclimatisation time. Seresafari strongly recommends the 6-day option, which adds a critical acclimatisation day at Horombo Hut and significantly improves summit success. We always advise longer routes (Machame or Lemosho) for the best summit experience.

Hut-by-Hut Itinerary (6 Days)
Day
1
Arusha → Marangu Gate → Mandara Hut
Marangu Gate (1,830m) → Mandara Hut (2,720m) | 3–4 hrs | 8 km

Transfer from Arusha to Marangu Gate (approximately 1.5 hours). Complete park registration, meet your mountain crew and begin trekking through Kilimanjaro's lush rainforest — a dense, green and birdsong-filled world. The trail is wide and well-maintained. Colobus and blue monkeys are frequently spotted in the canopy. Arrive at Mandara Hut — a collection of A-frame huts in a forest clearing — for dinner and your first night on the mountain. The huts sleep 2–4 people in comfortable bunk beds with mattresses provided.

Hut: Mandara Hut (2,720m) — A-Frame Dormitory Huts | All Meals
Day
2
Mandara Hut → Horombo Hut
Mandara Hut (2,720m) → Horombo Hut (3,720m) | 5–6 hrs | 12 km

Exit the rainforest and enter Kilimanjaro's heath and moorland zone — a dramatic transition as the trees thin and giant heather, groundsel and lobelia plants take over. The trail opens into broad, sweeping moorland with increasingly clear views of both Kibo (the main summit) and Mawenzi (the dramatic secondary peak). Arrive at Horombo Hut — Kilimanjaro's largest hut complex — with a dining hall, sleeping huts and an alpine sunset view that is genuinely spectacular. First signs of altitude may be noticed here — headache is common and normal.

Hut: Horombo Hut (3,720m) — Largest Hut Complex | All Meals
Day
3
Horombo Hut — Acclimatisation Day (Mawenzi Side Trip)
Horombo Hut (3,720m) → Mawenzi Tarn (4,330m) → Return to Horombo (3,720m) | 4–5 hrs

The crucial acclimatisation day on the 6-day option — this is the day that most improves summit success rates for Marangu climbers. Trek from Horombo toward the dramatic Mawenzi Peak — Kilimanjaro's secondary summit — reaching the Mawenzi Tarn glacial lake at 4,330m before returning to sleep at Horombo (3,720m). "Climb high, sleep low" — the same acclimatisation strategy as used on Machame and Lemosho. Spend the afternoon resting, hydrating and eating well. Your guide will brief you on the summit night strategy this evening.

Day
4
Horombo Hut → Kibo Hut
Horombo Hut (3,720m) → The Saddle → Kibo Hut (4,703m) | 5–6 hrs | 10 km

Cross "The Saddle" — a vast, high-altitude plateau between Mawenzi and Kibo peaks — a desolate, flat alpine desert that stretches several kilometres between the two volcanic summits. This is one of Kilimanjaro's most distinctive landscapes — completely barren, windswept and otherworldly, with the glaciated Kibo summit wall rising dramatically at the far end. Arrive at Kibo Hut — stone dormitory huts at 4,703m — for a hot meal and enforced rest. Sleep from 6 PM; midnight alarm. This is the highest hut on the mountain and the launch point for the summit.

Day
5
SUMMIT NIGHT → Uhuru Peak → Horombo Hut
Kibo Hut (4,703m) → Gillman's Point (5,685m) → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Horombo Hut (3,720m)

Midnight. The alarm sounds and you dress in every layer you own. The Marangu summit trail climbs steep, loose scree in a series of relentless switchbacks — Hans Meyer Cave at 5,150m is a checkpoint where many climbers feel the full force of altitude for the first time. Continue upward — the crater rim at Gillman's Point (5,685m) is 5–7 hours above. From Gillman's Point the additional traverse to Uhuru Peak (5,895m) takes another 45–60 minutes. Summit at sunrise. Africa below. Glaciers beside you. The world's greatest moment of personal achievement. Begin immediate descent — the long journey back to Horombo Hut takes the rest of the day. Descend at a steady pace; your legs will feel the 12+ km of elevation gain and loss.

Day
6
Horombo Hut → Marangu Gate → Arusha
Horombo Hut (3,720m) → Mandara Hut (2,720m) → Marangu Gate (1,830m) | 5–7 hrs | 20 km

Final descent through the full Marangu Route — moorland to rainforest — arriving at Marangu Gate where rangers award your official KINAPA Summit Certificate. The porter farewell ceremony is a joyful celebration of shared achievement. Transfer back to Arusha for celebration dinner, hot shower and the beginning of your summit story. Well done — you have stood on the Roof of Africa.

Huts provide dormitory sleeping with foam mattresses and pillows. You must bring your own sleeping bag (−15°C minimum). Huts are communal — privacy is limited. Separate hut rooms can sometimes be booked for couples at extra cost.
Important: Marangu vs Other Routes

Lower summit success rate: The standard 5-day Marangu has a summit success rate of approximately 65–75% — lower than Machame, Lemosho or Rongai. This is primarily due to insufficient acclimatisation time. The 6-day option (with extra Horombo night) improves this significantly. We always recommend 6 days minimum.

Hut advantages: Sleeping in huts rather than tents means warmer nights, more social atmosphere, and no tent setup/takedown. Huts also provide a dining table and chairs. For travellers who specifically do not want to sleep in tents, Marangu is the only option.

Same path up and down: Marangu is the only route that ascends and descends the same trail — meaning you see identical scenery both ways. Other routes (Machame, Lemosho, Rongai) offer a circuit with varied scenery on descent. This is a personal preference consideration.

Our recommendation: For the best summit experience, scenic variety and highest success rate, Seresafari recommends Machame (7 days) or Lemosho (8 days) over Marangu. However, if hut sleeping, shorter duration or budget is your priority, 6-day Marangu remains a worthwhile and achievable Kilimanjaro experience.
