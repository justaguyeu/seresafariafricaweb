Route Overview

The Machame Route — nicknamed the "Whiskey Route" — is Kilimanjaro's most popular and widely regarded as its most scenic. It approaches the mountain from the south-west, climbing through rich rainforest before ascending across the dramatic Southern Ice Fields toward Uhuru Peak via the Barafu or Lava Tower acclimatisation loop.

Machame offers exceptional landscape variety — from dense cloud forest dripping with moss, through giant heather moorland with views of Moshi below, across the alien alpine desert, and up the steep Western Breach or the standard Southern Corridor to the summit crater. Each day presents a completely different ecosystem and visual experience.

The route is technically straightforward but physically demanding — steeper and more challenging than Rongai or Marangu. The 7-day option adds a crucial acclimatisation day at Karanga Camp and significantly improves summit success rates. Seresafari recommends the 7-day option for most climbers.

Camp-by-Camp Itinerary (7 Days)
Day
1
Arusha → Machame Gate → Machame Camp
Machame Gate (1,800m) → Machame Camp (3,100m) | 5–7 hrs trekking | 11 km

Transfer from Arusha to Machame Gate (approximately 1.5 hours). Complete park registration, meet your mountain crew — lead guide, assistant guide, cook and porters — and begin trekking through lush tropical rainforest. The forest canopy shelters colobus monkeys, blue monkeys and hundreds of bird species. The trail is well-defined but muddy and steep in sections. Emerge from the forest above the cloud line as you approach Machame Camp on the forest edge. Dinner and acclimatisation briefing from your lead guide.

Rainforest Trek 5–7 Hours Walking Wildlife in Forest Above the Cloud Line
Camp: Machame Camp (3,100m) | All Meals Provided by Mountain Chef
Day
2
Machame Camp → Shira Camp
Machame Camp (3,100m) → Shira Camp (3,840m) | 5–7 hrs | 5 km

Trek out of the rainforest and into the heath and moorland zone — a dramatic transition where giant heather trees and groundsels replace the forest canopy. The trail climbs steeply across a rocky ridge with increasingly spectacular views back over the forest and forward toward Kibo's snow-capped summit. The Shira Plateau opens ahead — an ancient volcanic crater now a high-altitude wetland. Arrive at Shira Camp on the plateau with sweeping 360° views. Altitude begins to be felt here — drink plenty of water and eat well.

Heath & Moorland Zone First Kibo Summit Views Shira Plateau Arrival Acclimatisation Hydration
Camp: Shira Camp (3,840m) | All Meals
Day
3
Shira Camp → Lava Tower → Barranco Camp (Acclimatisation)
Shira (3,840m) → Lava Tower (4,630m) → Barranco Camp (3,950m) | 7–8 hrs | 10 km

The critical acclimatisation day — "climb high, sleep low." Trek across the Shira Plateau and climb to the Lava Tower at 4,630m — the highest you'll reach before summit night. The Lava Tower is a striking volcanic plug rising 300m from the plateau, a dramatic and alien landscape. Lunch at the tower before descending to Barranco Camp — significantly lower in altitude. This ascent and descent is the most important acclimatisation strategy on the route, dramatically improving summit success rates by conditioning your body to altitude without a permanent high-altitude stay. Arrive at Barranco Camp in the Barranco Valley with views of the impressive Barranco Wall ahead.

Lava Tower (4,630m) "Climb High Sleep Low" Strategy Critical Acclimatisation Day Southern Ice Fields in View
Camp: Barranco Camp (3,950m) | All Meals
Day
4
Barranco Camp → Karanga Camp
Barranco Camp (3,950m) → Karanga Camp (4,035m) | 4–5 hrs | 5 km

The famous Barranco Wall climb — a steep but exhilarating scramble up the 300m wall using both hands and feet on solid rock. Despite its dramatic appearance, the wall is not technical and requires no climbing equipment — just confidence and a head for heights. Your guides assist at every step. Views from the top of the wall across the Southern Ice Fields and the Breach Wall are extraordinary. After the wall, traverse across Kilimanjaro's southern face to Karanga Valley and climb to Karanga Camp — a well-positioned camp for the push to Barafu tomorrow.

Barranco Wall Scramble Ice Field Panoramas Southern Face Traverse Karanga Valley Crossing
Camp: Karanga Camp (4,035m) | All Meals
Day
5
Karanga Camp → Barafu Camp (High Camp)
Karanga Camp (4,035m) → Barafu Camp (4,673m) | 4–5 hrs | 4 km

A short but steep climb from Karanga to Barafu — the high camp and launch point for the summit push. The trail crosses increasingly barren alpine desert — nothing but volcanic rock, scree and dust. Arrive at Barafu by early afternoon. Your guide will brief you thoroughly on the summit night strategy: departure time (typically midnight), clothing layering, pace, and what to expect. Rest and sleep as much as possible between 5 PM and 11 PM — your alarm for summit night will come quickly. Eat a hot meal and stay warm. The summit is 1,200 vertical metres above your tent.

Camp: Barafu High Camp (4,673m) | All Meals · Depart midnight
Day
6
SUMMIT NIGHT → Uhuru Peak → Mweka Camp
Barafu (4,673m) → Stella Point (5,739m) → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp (3,100m) | 12–16 hrs total

The night that changes everything. Depart Barafu Camp at midnight (sometimes as early as 11:30 PM to allow for slower pacing). The darkness is complete save for headlamps and stars — the Milky Way above Kilimanjaro is breathtaking. The trail climbs endlessly upward through loose scree and volcanic rock — every step deliberate, your guide setting a "pole pole" (very slow) pace. After 5–6 hours of climbing, the crater rim at Stella Point (5,739m) appears — the hardest moment of the night. From Stella Point, a 45-minute walk around the crater rim leads to Uhuru Peak at 5,895m. Sunrise over Africa from the highest point on the continent. The emotion of this moment is indescribable. Allow 30–45 minutes at the summit for photos and celebration before the long descent. Down from Uhuru to Barafu for rest and a hot meal, then continue down the Mweka Route to Mweka Camp. A long but triumphant day.

Midnight Departure Milky Way Above Africa Stella Point (5,739m) Uhuru Peak (5,895m) at Sunrise Long Descent to Mweka Camp
Camp: Mweka Camp (3,100m) | All Meals — well-earned rest!
Day
7
Mweka Camp → Mweka Gate → Arusha (Celebration!)
Mweka Camp (3,100m) → Mweka Gate (1,640m) → Arusha | 3–4 hrs descent | 10 km

The final descent through Kilimanjaro's lush rainforest — a beautiful and emotional walk as the adrenaline of summit night fades into deep satisfaction. Colobus monkeys watch from the canopy as you wind downward through mossy forest. Reach Mweka Gate where certificates are awarded — your KINAPA Summit Certificate confirming your ascent. The traditional tipping ceremony for your mountain crew follows — a heartfelt gathering where guides and porters perform farewell songs. Transfer back to Arusha arriving early afternoon. Tonight: a celebratory dinner, a hot shower, and the knowledge that you have stood on the Roof of Africa.

Important Information

Physical requirements: No technical climbing experience needed, but strong cardiovascular fitness is essential. Train for 3–6 months before the climb with hiking, running or cycling. Day hikes with significant elevation gain are the best preparation.

Altitude sickness: AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) affects climbers at any fitness level. Common symptoms include headache, nausea and fatigue. Our guides monitor every climber twice daily with pulse oximeters. Emergency oxygen is carried at all times. We will never pressure climbers to continue if health is at risk.

Essential gear: Waterproof outer layer, thermal base layers (wool or synthetic), insulating mid-layer, warm gloves (summit nights reach -20°C), balaclava, waterproof boots, headlamp (with extra batteries), sunglasses with UV400 protection, and a sleeping bag rated to -15°C minimum.

Altitude medication: Acetazolamide (Diamox) can help with acclimatisation. Consult your doctor before the climb. Our guides are trained to advise on symptoms but do not prescribe medication. We always carry emergency oxygen and dexamethasone in severe cases.

Porter tipping: Tipping is an important part of the mountain crew's income. Recommended amounts: Lead Guide $20–25/day, Assistant Guide $15/day, Cook $15/day, Porters $10/day (per group). Tips are given at the formal farewell ceremony at Mweka Gate.

Insurance mandatory: All Kilimanjaro climbers must carry comprehensive travel insurance with medical evacuation cover. This is non-negotiable. Helicopter evacuation from altitude can cost $10,000–$50,000 without insurance. We verify insurance details before the climb begins.
