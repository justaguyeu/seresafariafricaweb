Overview

The Serengeti is Tanzania's most celebrated national park — a vast, ancient ecosystem of endless golden savannah, rocky kopjes, acacia woodland, and seasonal rivers stretching 14,763 km² across the northern interior. The name itself comes from the Maasai word Siringet, meaning "the place where the land runs on forever," and standing on the open plains at dawn, you understand exactly why.

The park is the stage for the Great Migration — the largest overland animal movement on Earth. Each year, over 1.5 million wildebeest, 200,000 zebras, and 500,000 Thomson's gazelles follow a continuous 3,000 km circuit dictated by rainfall, grass, and instinct. This ancient, relentless cycle of life and death plays out in spectacular river crossings, dramatic predator chases, and vast thundering herds that stretch to the horizon.

Beyond the Migration, the Serengeti supports one of the densest concentrations of predators in Africa. Lion prides roam the kopjes, leopards drape themselves over acacia branches, cheetahs sprint across open plains, and vast hyena clans patrol the night. All of Africa's Big Five are present throughout the year.

Park Highlights

Mara River Crossings — July to September, thousands of wildebeest plunge into crocodile-filled waters in one of nature's most breathtaking spectacles.

Calving Season — January to February on the Southern Plains. Up to 8,000 calves born daily, drawing the highest predator density anywhere in Africa.

Balloon Safaris — Float silently over the plains at sunrise, watching herds move below. Champagne breakfast in the bush follows — truly unforgettable.

Night Game Drives — The Serengeti after dark reveals nocturnal hunters: servals, genets, honey badgers and sleeping lions.

Birding Paradise — Over 500 bird species including secretary birds, kori bustards, lilac-breasted rollers, and vast flocks of raptors riding thermals.

Photography — Kopjes at golden hour, cheetah on a termite mound, lone acacia silhouetted against sunset — every frame is a masterpiece.

Park Zones & Areas

Seronera Valley
The park's heartland. Excellent year-round big cat sightings, Seronera River, and central accommodation hub.
Northern Serengeti
Lamai and Kogatende areas. Best for Mara River crossings July–October. Remote and exclusive.
Southern Plains (Ndutu)
Vast open grassland. Calving season Jan–Feb. Outstanding cheetah and lion sightings on the short grass.
Western Corridor
Grumeti River, hippo pools, and the first migration crossings in June–July. Fewer visitors, raw wilderness.

Important Information

Park Fees: USD $82 per person per day (international adult). All fees included in Seresafari Africa packages — no hidden extras.

Health: Yellow fever vaccination certificate required. Malaria prophylaxis strongly recommended. Consult your doctor 6–8 weeks before travel.

Climate: Two dry seasons (June–Oct and Jan–Feb). Average temperatures 20–28°C. Pack layers — mornings on the plains can be surprisingly cool.

Photography: Bring a telephoto lens (minimum 300mm). Memory cards, extra batteries, and a dust cover for your equipment are essential.
