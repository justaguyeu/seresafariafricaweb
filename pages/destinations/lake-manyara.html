Overview

Lake Manyara National Park is one of Tanzania's most surprising and diverse parks — compact in size, yet extraordinary in its variety of habitats, wildlife, and landscapes. Stretching along the floor of the Great Rift Valley beneath a dramatic 600-metre escarpment, the park encompasses dense groundwater forest, open savannah, acacia woodland, swamps, and the vast alkaline lake itself — each zone supporting different and remarkable wildlife communities.

The park's most famous claim to fame is its tree-climbing lions — a behaviour documented nowhere else in East Africa. For reasons debated by scientists (possibly to escape flies, to catch a breeze, or simply habit), Manyara's lions regularly drape themselves in the branches of massive fig and acacia trees. Spotting a lion 5 metres up in a tree is an unforgettable safari moment.

The alkaline lake supports immense flocks of flamingos — often numbering in the hundreds of thousands — alongside pelicans, storks, spoonbills, and over 400 other bird species. For birdwatchers, Lake Manyara is one of Tanzania's premier destinations.

Park Highlights

Tree-Climbing Lions — Manyara's most celebrated phenomenon. Ask your guide to take you to the fever tree forests where these lions spend their mornings resting in the canopy.

Flamingo Flocks — When conditions are right, the lake's shoreline turns pink with thousands of lesser and greater flamingos. A spectacle of colour and sound.

Hippo Pools — A series of pools near the park entrance host vast numbers of hippos. Watching them surface, snort, and fight is endlessly entertaining.

Groundwater Forest — A dense, primeval forest fed by springs from the escarpment. Home to troops of blue monkeys, baboons, and foraging elephants in cool shade.

Rift Valley Views — The drive from Mto wa Mbu through the escarpment offers jaw-dropping panoramas across the valley floor — a perfect photography stop.

Cycling & Canopy Walks — Some operators offer cycling through the village and treetop canopy walkways for a completely different perspective on the park.

Practical Information

Location: Just 126 km from Arusha (1.5 hours). The perfect first or last stop on a Northern Circuit safari — easy access, huge rewards.

Duration: A half-day is possible but a full day allows you to cover all habitat zones — forest, grassland, and lakeshore — and find the tree-climbing lions.

Birding Tip: Bring good binoculars and a checklist. The transition zones between forest and open land are outstanding — early morning is best for bird activity.

Nearby: Mto wa Mbu village at the park entrance is a vibrant market town worth an hour's wander — buy local crafts, bananas, and meet the residents of this culturally mixed community.
