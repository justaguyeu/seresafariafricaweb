Overview

Zanzibar is a dream island destination like no other — a magical convergence of African, Arab, Indian, and European cultures washed by the warm, impossibly turquoise waters of the Indian Ocean. The Zanzibar Archipelago lies 35 kilometres off the coast of mainland Tanzania, and from the moment you step off the plane into the fragrant, spice-scented air, it feels entirely removed from the world you left behind.

The island's capital, Stone Town, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of East Africa's most extraordinary cities — a labyrinth of narrow coral-stone alleys, ornate carved wooden doorways, lively spice markets, and ancient Arab palaces layered over one another in centuries of history. The legacy of the Omani Sultanate, the slave trade era, and the colonial period are all written into the city's walls.

Beyond Stone Town, Zanzibar's beaches are among the finest in Africa — from the calm, shallow turquoise lagoons of Nungwi in the north to the kite-surfing capital of Paje on the east coast, and the intimate, exclusive resorts of the south. The coral reef ecosystem surrounding the island is rich with life, supporting manta rays, whale sharks, sea turtles, dolphins, and thousands of tropical fish species.

Island Highlights

Stone Town — UNESCO World Heritage Site. Wander narrow alleys, visit the former Slave Market, watch dhows in the harbour at sunset, and eat at the famous Forodhani Gardens night food market.

Whale Shark Snorkelling — Between October and March, whale sharks gather around Diani and Mafia Island channels. Swimming with Earth's largest fish is a once-in-a-lifetime experience.

Spice Farm Tours — Zanzibar's original wealth came from cloves, cinnamon, cardamom, and vanilla. Guided spice farm tours let you taste, smell, and buy directly from growers.

Diving & Snorkelling — The Mnemba Atoll offers some of the Indian Ocean's finest diving — walls covered in soft coral, sea turtles, octopus, and rainbow reef fish.

Dhow Sunset Cruise — Sail into the sunset on a traditional wooden dhow, champagne in hand, watching Stone Town's silhouette turn gold against the dusk. Quintessential Zanzibar.

Kitesurfing at Paje — The shallow lagoon at Paje Beach, with its reliable south-east trade winds, is one of the world's top kitesurfing destinations. Lessons available for beginners.

Travel Tips

Getting There: Direct flights to Zanzibar (ZNZ) from Kilimanjaro (JRO) and Dar es Salaam (DAR). Many safaris end with a Zanzibar beach extension — the perfect combination.

Dress Code: Zanzibar is a Muslim-majority island. Cover shoulders and knees when in Stone Town and villages. Swimwear is appropriate only on beach resort areas.

Best Season: June–October (dry, less humid) and December–February (warm, clear seas) are ideal. Avoid April–May (long rains). October–March for whale sharks.

Food: Don't miss the Forodhani Gardens night market in Stone Town — fresh lobster, octopus, and Zanzibar pizza. Also try the local specialty: coconut rice with mchuzi wa samaki (fish curry).
