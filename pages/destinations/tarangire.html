Overview

Tarangire National Park is Tanzania's best-kept secret — a breathtaking, wild landscape that most visitors bypass in favour of the Serengeti and Ngorongoro, yet which rewards those who make the journey with one of the most intimate and extraordinary safari experiences in East Africa.

The park's defining feature is its ancient landscape of enormous baobab trees — some over 1,000 years old — whose massive, contorted forms rise from the golden grass like something from a dream. These iconic trees frame every game drive, creating an otherworldly beauty that is uniquely Tarangire's own.

During the dry season (June–October), the Tarangire River becomes the only permanent water source for hundreds of kilometres. Wildlife from across the Maasai Steppe converges here in extraordinary numbers, with elephant herds of 300 or more animals being an almost daily occurrence. The density of elephants makes Tarangire Africa's premier elephant destination — and they are invariably relaxed, accustomed to vehicles, and astonishing to observe.

What Makes Tarangire Unique

Elephant Herds — Africa's largest elephant concentrations during dry season. Herds of 200–400 animals are common. Young calves, matriarchs, and enormous bulls make for extraordinary watching.

Baobab Landscapes — Thousands of ancient baobabs scattered across the park create unrivalled photographic scenery. The "Tree of Life" supports dozens of species including mongoose, hornbills, and pythons.

Walking Safaris — The park's remoter zones permit guided walking safaris — experience Africa on foot, tracking animals and reading the bush with expert Maasai and professional guides.

Night Drives — Some camps offer nocturnal game drives revealing the park's secret life: aardvark, civet, genet, bush baby, and hunting lions invisible in daylight.

World-Class Birding — Over 550 species including the rare ashy starling, yellow-collared lovebird, and vast flocks of ground hornbills. Serious birders treat Tarangire as a pilgrimage site.

Solitude — Far fewer visitors than the Serengeti. You may share a game drive area with just one or two other vehicles — or none at all. A profoundly different experience from the famous parks.

Good to Know

Getting There: 2 hours' drive from Arusha on good tar road. Often combined as the first stop on a Northern Circuit safari. No need to fly.

Where to Stay: Tented camps inside or just outside the park provide the best wildlife access. We have hand-picked the finest camps along the river.

Climate: Lower altitude than Ngorongoro — warm and dry in season. Temperatures 25–32°C during the day. Cool evenings. Pack light but bring a layer.

Best Combined With: Lake Manyara (just 1.5 hrs away) and Ngorongoro Crater — the perfect 5-day Northern Circuit opening act.
