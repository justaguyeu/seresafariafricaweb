Northern Tanzania UNESCO Heritage

Serengeti National Park

Tanzania's most celebrated park covers 14,763 km² of endless golden savannah, acacia woodland and rocky kopjes. It is home to the world-famous Great Migration — the annual movement of over 1.5 million wildebeest, 200,000 zebras and 500,000 gazelles in a continuous cycle driven by rainfall and fresh grass. The Serengeti hosts all of the Big Five and over 500 bird species.

Best time: June–October (dry season) & Jan–Feb (calving)
Wildlife: Wildebeest, lion, leopard, cheetah, elephant, buffalo, rhino
Experience: Great Migration, balloon safaris, game drives
Stay: Tented camps inside the park for best wildlife access
Conservation Area UNESCO Heritage

Ngorongoro Crater

The world's largest intact volcanic caldera, Ngorongoro is a self-contained natural world 19 km across and 600 m deep. The crater floor supports over 25,000 mammals, including one of Africa's densest lion populations and a significant black rhino population. Its unique geography means wildlife rarely leaves, giving guests extraordinary wildlife concentration in a single dramatic landscape.

Best time: Year-round — wildlife density is consistently exceptional
Wildlife: All Big Five including rare black rhino, flamingos
Experience: Full-day crater floor game drive, Maasai visits
Stay: Crater rim lodges with spectacular sunrise views
Northern Tanzania Elephant Capital

Tarangire National Park

Tarangire is Tanzania's hidden gem — an extraordinary landscape of ancient baobab trees, seasonal swamps, and the permanent Tarangire River that acts as a magnet for wildlife during the dry season. Home to Africa's largest elephant herds (often 300+ in a single group), Tarangire also supports oryx, eland, and the striking fringe-eared oryx. The park sees far fewer tourists than Serengeti, offering an intimate safari experience.

Best time: June–October (dry season, peak wildlife concentration)
Wildlife: Elephants, lions, leopards, oryx, wildebeest, zebra
Experience: Baobab landscapes, walking safaris, night drives
Stay: Tented camps near the river for best elephant sightings
Rift Valley 400+ Bird Species

Lake Manyara

Compact but incredibly diverse, Lake Manyara National Park stretches along the base of the dramatic Rift Valley escarpment. The alkaline lake draws flamingos in flamboyant pink flocks, pelicans, storks, and over 400 other bird species. Manyara is also famous for its legendary tree-climbing lions — a behaviour unique to this park — as well as large herds of elephants, hippos in the lake shallows, and a dense forest canopy alive with monkeys and baboons.

Best time: June–October; flamingos best Nov–June
Wildlife: Tree-climbing lions, flamingos, elephants, hippos
Experience: Birdwatching, forest drives, tree-climbing lion search
Stay: Lodges on the escarpment with panoramic lake views
Indian Ocean Island Beach Paradise

Zanzibar Archipelago

Just 35 minutes by flight from Dar es Salaam, the island of Zanzibar combines turquoise Indian Ocean waters, powder-white beaches, rich Swahili culture, and a fascinating Arab trading history. Stone Town — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — is a labyrinth of narrow alleys, carved doorways, and spice-scented markets. The island's coral reefs shelter sea turtles, whale sharks, and abundant tropical fish, making it a world-class diving and snorkelling destination.

Best time: June–October & December–February (dry seasons)
Activities: Diving, snorkelling, spice tours, Stone Town walks
Highlights: Sea turtles, whale sharks, coral reefs, sunsets
Stay: Boutique beach resorts from Nungwi to Paje
Northern Tanzania 5,895 m Summit

Mount Kilimanjaro

Africa's highest point and the world's tallest free-standing mountain, Kilimanjaro is a dormant stratovolcano rising dramatically from the surrounding plains to 5,895 metres. The mountain is accessible to non-technical climbers via several established routes, each passing through five distinct ecological zones — from tropical rainforest through heath and moorland to the barren alpine desert and finally the glaciated summit. No ropes or technical climbing skills required, but strong fitness and acclimatisation are essential.

Best time: January–March & June–October (dry, clear skies)
Routes: Machame, Lemosho, Rongai, Marangu, Umbwe
Duration: 5–9 days depending on route and acclimatisation
Success rate: 95% with Seresafari's expert guides

Best Time to Visit Tanzania

Tanzania is a year-round destination. Each season offers unique wildlife experiences — here's how to choose your perfect timing.

June – October
Dry Season · Peak Safari

The best time for wildlife viewing. Animals congregate around water sources, vegetation is low (easier spotting), and the Great Migration river crossings happen in the Mara River (July–September). Minimal rain, comfortable temperatures, and excellent road conditions throughout the parks.

November – December
Short Rains · Green Season

Short rains bring lush green landscapes and fewer crowds. Excellent birdwatching as migratory species arrive. Prices are lower and lodges less busy, giving you a more intimate experience. Wildebeest begin their southward migration toward the Serengeti's southern plains for calving season.

January – February
Calving Season · Predator Action

The Great Wildebeest Calving season unfolds on the Southern Serengeti plains. Up to 8,000 calves are born each day, attracting an extraordinary concentration of predators — cheetahs, lions, leopards, hyenas and wild dogs. It's an incredible, raw wildlife spectacle unlike anything else on Earth.
